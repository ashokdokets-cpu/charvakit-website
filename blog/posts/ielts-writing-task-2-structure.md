# IELTS Writing Task 2: The Structure That Scores Band 7+

IELTS Writing Task 2 is a 250-word essay written in 40 minutes. It accounts for two-thirds of your Writing band score.

Most test-takers plateau around Band 5.5-6.0 — not because of weak English, but because of **weak structure**. Examiners reward clear, argumentative essays with a predictable shape. If you learn the shape, your score jumps.

This guide gives you a proven structure that repeatedly scores Band 7+ — plus the exact criteria examiners use.

## The 4 Criteria (What You're Actually Scored On)

IELTS Writing Task 2 is scored on 4 criteria, each on a 0-9 band scale:

### 1. Task Response (25%)

**What it measures:** Did you answer the question fully?

**Band 7+ requires:**
- A clear position throughout the essay
- All parts of the prompt addressed
- Main ideas developed with examples

**Common mistakes:**
- Not answering the actual question
- Giving a one-sided view when asked to discuss both sides
- Under-developing arguments (a single sentence isn't enough)

### 2. Coherence and Cohesion (25%)

**What it measures:** How well your ideas connect and flow.

**Band 7+ requires:**
- Clear paragraphing (usually 4 paragraphs)
- Logical progression of ideas
- Effective use of linking words (not overused)

**Common mistakes:**
- One giant paragraph
- Overusing "moreover" and "furthermore" in every sentence
- Linking words that don't fit the logic

### 3. Lexical Resource (25%)

**What it measures:** Range and precision of vocabulary.

**Band 7+ requires:**
- Varied word choice
- Some less common vocabulary used accurately
- Paraphrasing of the prompt

**Common mistakes:**
- Repeating the same words
- Using very formal words incorrectly
- Copying phrases from the prompt without paraphrasing

### 4. Grammatical Range and Accuracy (25%)

**What it measures:** Sentence variety and correctness.

**Band 7+ requires:**
- Mix of simple, compound, and complex sentences
- Predominantly error-free
- Correct use of tense, articles, prepositions

**Common mistakes:**
- Only using simple sentences
- Systematic errors (missing articles, wrong tense)
- Run-on sentences

## The 4-Paragraph Structure

For **any** Task 2 essay — whether it's opinion, discussion, or problem-solution — this structure works.

### Paragraph 1 — Introduction (2-3 sentences)

**Goal:** Paraphrase the prompt and state your position.

**Structure:**
1. Paraphrase the question in your own words
2. State your position (or introduce both sides if discussing)
3. Optional: preview your main arguments

**Example (Opinion essay):**

**Prompt:** "Some people believe that technology has made our lives more complicated. To what extent do you agree or disagree?"

**Introduction:**
> "While some argue that technology has complicated daily life, I believe it has overwhelmingly simplified modern living. This essay will discuss how technology has improved communication, efficiency, and access to information."

**Why this works:**
- Paraphrases the prompt
- Clear position stated
- Previews 3 arguments

### Paragraph 2 — First Main Point

**Goal:** Develop one clear argument with evidence.

**Structure:**
1. Topic sentence (state the argument)
2. Explanation (why it's true)
3. Example (specific, concrete)
4. Link back to the main idea

**Example:**
> "Firstly, technology has dramatically improved global communication. Platforms like WhatsApp, Zoom, and email allow individuals to reach anyone, anywhere, in seconds. For example, migrant workers in the Gulf can now video call their families in India daily — something impossible 20 years ago. This instant connectivity has strengthened relationships that distance once threatened."

**Why this works:**
- Clear topic sentence
- Explains the argument
- Concrete example
- Reflects on significance

### Paragraph 3 — Second Main Point

Same structure as paragraph 2. **Different argument.**

**Example:**
> "Secondly, technology has streamlined countless daily tasks. Online banking, e-commerce, and food delivery apps save hours of time that would otherwise be spent queuing, shopping, and cooking. In urban India, for instance, a professional can now complete a week's errands in a single evening. This efficiency gain has freed time for family, leisure, and personal development."

### Paragraph 4 — Conclusion (2 sentences)

**Goal:** Summarize your position without introducing new ideas.

**Structure:**
1. Restate your position (rephrased)
2. Summarize your main points

**Example:**
> "In conclusion, technology has simplified rather than complicated modern life. By improving communication, efficiency, and access to information, it has made daily living easier and more connected. Any complications are minor trade-offs against these significant benefits."

## Full Essay Example (Band 7-8 Range)

**Total: 273 words**

> **Prompt:** Some people believe that technology has made our lives more complicated. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
>
> While some argue that technology has complicated daily life, I believe it has overwhelmingly simplified modern living. This essay will discuss how technology has improved communication, efficiency, and access to information.
>
> Firstly, technology has dramatically improved global communication. Platforms like WhatsApp, Zoom, and email allow individuals to reach anyone, anywhere, in seconds. For example, migrant workers in the Gulf can now video call their families in India daily — something impossible 20 years ago. This instant connectivity has strengthened relationships that distance once threatened.
>
> Secondly, technology has streamlined countless daily tasks. Online banking, e-commerce, and food delivery apps save hours of time that would otherwise be spent queuing, shopping, and cooking. In urban India, a professional can now complete a week's errands in a single evening. This efficiency gain has freed time for family, leisure, and personal development.
>
> Finally, technology has democratized access to information. Search engines, online courses, and digital libraries place decades of human knowledge at our fingertips. A student in a small town can now access the same lectures as a student in Delhi. This levelling of educational opportunity has profound long-term benefits.
>
> In conclusion, technology has simplified rather than complicated modern life. By improving communication, efficiency, and access to information, it has made daily living easier and more connected. Any complications are minor trade-offs against these significant benefits.

## Common Band 5-6 Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)

**1. The "I think" opening**

❌ "I think technology is good. I will discuss..."
✅ Direct position: "While some argue..., I believe..."

**2. One-sided on "Discuss both views"**

If the prompt says "discuss both views and give your own opinion", you must address BOTH sides. Many test-takers only argue one side and lose Task Response marks.

**3. No examples**

Every body paragraph needs a concrete example. Not hypothetical ("if someone were to..."). Real or plausible-specific.

**4. Repetitive linking**

If every sentence starts with "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally", your Coherence score drops. Use linking **between ideas**, not every sentence.

**5. Word count panic**

Under 250 words → Task Response capped at Band 5. Over 300 → you're wasting time. Aim for 260-280 words.
