# IELTS vs TOEFL: Which Should You Take in 2026?

If you're planning to study abroad, migrate, or work in an English-speaking country, you'll almost certainly need to prove your English proficiency. The two most widely accepted tests are **IELTS** (International English Language Testing System) and **TOEFL** (Test of English as a Foreign Language).

Both are accepted by thousands of universities and immigration authorities worldwide. But they're not identical, and the one you pick can affect your score, your preparation timeline, and even your visa.

This guide breaks down the differences in plain language so you can decide which test is right for you.

## The Short Answer

- **IELTS** is preferred by the UK, Australia, Canada, and New Zealand for immigration and study. It's also more common in India.
- **TOEFL** is dominant in the United States, though most US universities now accept both.
- **For Indian test-takers:** IELTS is the more common choice. Test centres are more widely available and the format feels familiar if you studied in an Indian curriculum.

If you're not sure, check the specific requirements of the university or immigration authority you're targeting. Most publish their preference clearly.

## Format Differences

### IELTS

- **Listening:** 30 minutes, 40 questions
- **Reading:** 60 minutes, 40 questions (Academic or General Training)
- **Writing:** 60 minutes, 2 tasks (Task 1: 150 words, Task 2: 250 words)
- **Speaking:** 11-14 minutes, face-to-face with an examiner
- **Total:** ~2 hours 45 minutes

**Score scale:** 0-9 band, in 0.5 increments.

### TOEFL iBT

- **Reading:** 54-72 minutes, 30-40 questions
- **Listening:** 41-57 minutes, 28-39 questions
- **Speaking:** 17 minutes, 4 tasks (recorded, no live examiner)
- **Writing:** 50 minutes, 2 tasks
- **Total:** ~3 hours

**Score scale:** 0-120, with each section out of 30.

## Key Differences That Matter

### 1. Speaking format

IELTS Speaking is a **live, face-to-face interview** with a trained examiner. You respond to questions in real time, and the examiner can probe, clarify, and adjust.

TOEFL Speaking is **recorded**. You speak into a microphone, and your responses are later scored by AI and human raters.

**Which is easier?** It depends. If you're comfortable in spontaneous conversation, IELTS feels natural. If you prefer preparing structured responses and speaking without an audience, TOEFL is easier.

### 2. Accent and listening

IELTS Listening includes **British, Australian, New Zealand, and American accents** — often within the same section. If your ear is trained to British English, IELTS will feel fine. If you've mainly heard American English, TOEFL is easier.

### 3. Writing style

IELTS Writing rewards **formal academic English** with clear structure. Task 2 expects a clear position, developed arguments, and a strong conclusion.

TOEFL Writing rewards **clear, direct English**. The integrated task requires you to summarize and respond to source material.

### 4. Score reporting

- IELTS: 13 days from test date (paper), 3-5 days (computer-delivered)
- TOEFL: 4-8 days from test date

Both are similar. IELTS has a slight edge if you take the computer-delivered version.

## Fees (2026)

- **IELTS:** ₹17,000 (India), ~$245 (US)
- **TOEFL:** ₹16,900 (India), ~$220 (US)

Fees are close. Budget for one retake — most test-takers need two attempts to reach their target score.

## Which Should You Take?

**Take IELTS if:**
- You're applying to the UK, Australia, Canada, or New Zealand
- You need a visa for immigration purposes
- You're comfortable with live conversation
- You've prepared mostly with British English materials

**Take TOEFL if:**
- You're applying to US universities (especially those that prefer TOEFL)
- You prefer structured, recorded speaking over live interviews
- Your ear is trained to American English
- You want a single-number score rather than a band

**Take whichever is required by your target.** If both are accepted, IELTS is usually the safer bet for Indian test-takers.

## How to Prepare

Both tests reward **consistent practice over cramming**. A typical 8-week plan:

- **Weeks 1-2:** Diagnostic test to find your baseline. Fix your weakest section.
- **Weeks 3-5:** Daily practice on all 4 sections. Simulate test conditions.
- **Weeks 6-7:** Full-length mock tests twice a week.
- **Week 8:** Light review, rest, and test day.
