## The 4 IELTS Speaking Criteria

Before we talk about practice, understand what's being scored. IELTS Speaking is rated on 4 criteria, each on a 0-9 band scale:

### 1. Fluency and Coherence

**What it measures:** How smoothly you speak, and how well your ideas connect.

**What examiners listen for:**
- Speaking at a natural pace without long pauses
- Using linking words ("however", "moreover", "on the other hand")
- Developing ideas without abandoning them halfway

**Common mistakes:** Memorized answers, speaking too fast to hide uncertainty, using "and" as the only connector.

### 2. Lexical Resource (Vocabulary)

**What it measures:** The range and precision of your vocabulary.

**What examiners listen for:**
- Varied word choice (not repeating "very good" or "big problem")
- Collocations and idioms used naturally
- Paraphrasing when you don't know the exact word

**Common mistakes:** Using very formal vocabulary in a casual context, relying on filler words, mispronouncing fancy words.

### 3. Grammatical Range and Accuracy

**What it measures:** The variety of sentence structures you use and how correct they are.

**What examiners listen for:**
- Mix of simple, compound, and complex sentences
- Correct use of tenses, articles, prepositions
- Conditional forms ("If I had more time, I would...")

**Common mistakes:** Only using present tense, dropping articles ("I went to market"), errors in third-person singular.

### 4. Pronunciation

**What it measures:** How easy it is for a listener to understand you.

**What examiners listen for:**
- Clear consonant and vowel sounds
- Correct word stress (e.g., "PREsent" vs "preSENT")
- Natural intonation (not flat or monotone)

**Common mistakes:** Speaking too softly, over-pronouncing every syllable, letting first-language accent interfere with clarity.

## The 3 Parts of IELTS Speaking

### Part 1 — Introduction and Interview (4-5 minutes)

The examiner asks you general questions about familiar topics:
- Where you're from
- Your work or studies
- Your home
- Daily routines
- Hobbies and interests

**What to aim for:** 2-3 sentences per answer. Not one-liners. Not essays. Just enough to show natural conversation.

**Example question:** "Where are you from?"

- ❌ **Bad:** "I am from Mumbai."
- ✅ **Good:** "I'm from Mumbai, which is a large city on the west coast of India. It's known for its film industry and its busy street markets."

### Part 2 — Long Turn (3-4 minutes)

You get a cue card with a topic. You have 1 minute to prepare, then speak for up to 2 minutes.

**Example cue card:**
> Describe a memorable journey you have taken.
> You should say:
> - Where you went
> - Who you went with
> - What you did there
> - Why it was memorable

**What to aim for:** A structured narrative. Begin with a broad statement, then add specific details, then reflect on why it mattered.

**Common mistake:** Panicking and finishing in 30 seconds. If you run out of things to say, add context, not filler.

### Part 3 — Discussion (4-5 minutes)

The examiner asks abstract questions related to the Part 2 topic. These are designed to test your ability to reason and argue.

**Example questions (if Part 2 was about a journey):**
- Why do people travel?
- How has travel changed in your country over the last 20 years?
- Do you think travel will become more or less common in the future?

**What to aim for:** Clear opinions with reasons. Use "because", "for example", "on the other hand".

## A 4-Week Practice Plan

### Week 1 — Diagnostic

- Take 2 full speaking tests.
- Note your lowest-scoring criterion.
- Read the feedback carefully, not just the band score.

### Week 2 — Weakness focus

- Practice 4-5 sessions focused on your weakest criterion.
- If it's Fluency, speak longer without stopping.
- If it's Lexical Resource, expand vocabulary on common topics.
- If it's Grammar, drill specific structures (conditionals, relative clauses).
- If it's Pronunciation, record and listen back.

### Week 3 — Full simulations

- 3 full 3-part tests per week.
- Simulate test conditions: quiet room, no interruptions, timer on.
- Target a 0.5 band improvement from Week 1.

### Week 4 — Polish

- Light practice only. 1-2 full tests.
- Review your best answers to build confidence.
- Rest before test day.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**1. Memorizing answers**

Examiners detect memorized responses instantly. You'll lose marks for Fluency. Answer naturally, even if slightly imperfect.

**2. Speaking too fast**

Fast speech isn't fluent speech. Aim for a natural, controlled pace.

**3. Ignoring Part 2 preparation time**

Use the 1 minute. Write 4-5 bullet points. Don't just stare at the card.

**4. Long silences in Part 3**

If you need a moment to think, use filler phrases: "That's an interesting question, let me think..." — this is normal and doesn't hurt your score.

**5. Using rare words incorrectly**

Precision beats rarity. "Big" used correctly scores higher than "prodigious" used wrong.
